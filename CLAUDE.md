# Deadset – Prototype

## What this is

Deadset is a first-person wave shooter in the spirit of Killing Floor 1 and 2, set in a mutated, post-apocalyptic 1980s Australia. Mutant pests and native wildlife attack in waves; the player fights back with repurposed tradie tools.

This repo is a **prototype only**. Its single job is to answer: *is the core loop fun?* The full design (heroes, enemy roster, upgrade tags, lore) lives in a separate design note and is deliberately out of scope here.

The previous project failed because too many systems buried the fun. **Keep this lean.** If a feature isn't listed under Prototype scope, don't build it. Suggest it instead.

## Tech

- Godot 4.6 (.NET build), C#, desktop (Windows first).
- Art is stylised low-poly made in-repo, no third-party asset imports:
  - Enemy models are generated by Blender scripts in `tools/blender/` (1.5k–3k vertices each, rigid parts).
  - The look is the "PosterDusk" cel style (`resources/styles/`), applied at runtime by `StyleApplier`; the sky moves towards dusk as waves go on.
  - Sounds are generated in code (`PlaceholderSfx`).
- Keyboard and mouse controls.
- Solo play only. Don't add networking, but avoid choices that would make co-op impossible later (for example, keep game state out of UI scripts).
- Performance target: up to 200 enemies alive at once.

## Prototype scope

1. **Arena:** one enclosed map with cover, lanes and spawn points around the edges, big enough that a horde has room to come at you.
2. **Player (The Tradie):** first-person movement (walk, sprint, jump), mouse look, health, armour, footsteps.
3. **Weapons:** they must feel punchy: recoil, muzzle flash, hit feedback, sound.
   - Nail Gun (starting weapon): projectile, fire rate, magazine and reload.
   - Brushcutter and Rivet Gun, bought in the trader. Switch with 1/2/3 or the mouse wheel.
   - Per-weapon mods bought in the trader, defined as data. Some stack (for example, pierce count and damage kept after a pierce).
4. **Enemies:**
   - **Emus (swarm):** fast, fragile, come in flocks, chase and peck. Mutated glowing heads sit at about eye height.
   - **Wombats (charger):** tanky; wind up, then charge in a straight line that the player can sidestep.
   - A few roster specials mixed into later waves: dingoes, magpies, kangaroos, cassowaries. The other roster models exist for testing only (Ctrl + number keys).
   - At most 6 enemies attack at once; the rest wait at reach.
5. **Waves:** a wave director spawns escalating waves (up to 200 alive), then a short break between them.
6. **Trader break:** during the break, the player spends cash earned from kills on ammo, armour, weapons and weapon mods, then **picks 1 of 3 random upgrades**.
7. **Upgrades (about 6):** player-level, apply to every weapon carried. Examples: Smoko Shield (damage resistance at low health), Hot Chips & Gravy (heal on wave end), Quick Loader (faster reload), Bouncing Shot (shots ricochet once), Oi! Watch It! (dash), Thongs Off (sprint boost).
8. **UI:** health, armour, ammo and weapon name, wave number, cash, hit-direction markers, the trader screen with the upgrade picker, and a game-over screen with restart.

## Design rules

- **Common enemies only need horizontal aim.** Swarm and fodder enemies keep their heads at about player eye height (1.4–1.7 m; the camera is at 1.6 m), so the crosshair can rest at head height like Killing Floor. Low, tall or flying enemies are rare specials that deliberately break the rhythm.
- **Heads are the skill target.** Head hits deal extra damage (one nail kills an emu; two to the body).
- **Each weapon has one clear job.** The nail gun is precise single-target fire, the brushcutter clears what's at arm's reach, and the rivet gun punches through lines. Mods deepen a weapon's job rather than turning one weapon into all of them.

## Architecture rules

- Keep weapon behaviour separate from player input. The player tells the equipped weapon to fire; the weapon decides how. New firing types (hitscan, cone, beam, melee, thrown) slot in without touching player code.
- Define upgrades and weapon mods as data (Godot `Resource`s) that modify stats or hook into events. Adding one should not require editing the player class.
- Define enemies with shared health and damage components plus a per-type behaviour script.
- Use signals and events for game flow (wave started, enemy killed, wave cleared) rather than direct cross-references.
- Keep tunable numbers (speeds, damage, wave sizes, prices) in exported properties or resources, not hard-coded.

## Structure

```
res://
├── scenes/        (Main, Arena, Player, enemies, weapons, UI)
├── scripts/
│   ├── Combat/    (health, weak spots, melee, hit reactions)
│   ├── Core/      (game events, run stats)
│   ├── Economy/   (wallet, trader)
│   ├── Effects/   (sparks, corpses, generated sounds)
│   ├── Enemies/
│   ├── Player/
│   ├── UI/
│   ├── Upgrades/
│   ├── Visuals/   (cel style)
│   ├── Waves/
│   └── Weapons/
├── resources/     (enemy stats, waves, upgrades, weapons and mods, styles)
├── assets/models/ (generated enemy models)
└── tools/blender/ (model generator scripts)
```
